## domain_models/krrood_spatial_math/src/krrood_spatial_math/coordinate_system.py:98 (inner product)
https://github.com/cram2/cognitive_robot_abstract_machine/pull/1019#discussion_r4668782904

**Claude:** Moved in 65dbd601. The inner product belongs to the Euclidean space: `EuclideanSpace.inner_product` computes uᵀGv. A frame only states its Gram matrix G, the inner products of its axis vectors in its own coordinates. For a `CartesianFrame`, G is the identity. For an affine frame such as a pixel frame it is unknown unless the pixel size is given. A vector of a space that is not Euclidean has no inner product at all (`SpaceMeasuresNoLengthsError`). The symbolic vectors compute the same product as one casadi expression.

## domain_models/krrood_spatial_math/src/krrood_spatial_math/cartesian_frame_3_dimensional.py:39 (embedded planes)
https://github.com/cram2/cognitive_robot_abstract_machine/pull/1019#discussion_r4665948300

**Claude:** Your instinct was right, and measuring showed a stronger reason than cost. The old check read "these frames share a plane" from forward kinematics at the current state:
- it cost 210 µs per planar change;
- `map → base` raised as soon as the drive's IMU reported a pitch of 1e-5 rad;
- a change built while a joint about x was at 0 stayed valid after the joint moved.

e575d846 makes 2D work like 3D. A planar change only checks that both frames' `embedded_plane`s are the same, by identity, as a 3D change compares spaces (now 9.5 µs).
- **Where a frame's plane comes from:** its structure, for every state. A body lies on its parent's plane when the connection keeps it there for all joint values. That is read from the connection's expression: the child's z-axis and origin height must be constants. A drive, a joint about z and a prismatic joint along x or y keep the plane; a 6-DOF connection or a joint about x does not.
- **Drives:** a drive always keeps its base on the map's plane; the roll and pitch it measures tilt the base without taking it off the plane.
- **Caching:** the plane is memoized per world revision.
- **Removed:** `EmbeddedPlaneRelation`, the UNKNOWN path and `MirroredPlaneFramesError`.

On 1D and 4D: a 2D frame still sits on a 3D frame's x-y plane, because every planar value here is given relative to a body. Getting from 3D to 2D stays an explicit projection (`EmbeddedPlaneProjection`, a `SpaceMap`), and a lower-dimensional space would relate to a higher one the same way.
